# Oliver the Good Boy — Digital Graphical Equity

## Purpose

This system turns **Oliver’s personality into recognisable graphic cues**, so the brand still feels like Oliver when the full mascot is not visible. It is designed to make the website and social output feel coherent, distinctive, warm, and immediately identifiable, while keeping the elements simple enough to translate to future merchandise.

> **Creative principle:** Oliver is not only an illustration; he is a set of ownable signals—his friendly portrait, orange tag, wagging movement, soft organic forms, and warm grey palette.

## The Equity System

| Element | Visual definition | Primary job | Recommended use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Oliver Portrait Crop** | Oliver’s head or upper body, framed by a charcoal circular porthole and allowed to overlap the edge | Creates the fastest human connection and recognisability | Hero artwork, social posts, avatar moments, loading states, feature-section anchors |
| **Orange Tag** | A simplified orange shield based on Oliver’s collar tag; it may be blank or carry an approved internal symbol | Functions as a compact recognisable signifier at small sizes | Favicon/app icon direction, bullet marker, card corner, sticker, CTA accent, later merch patch |
| **Tail-Swish Ribbon** | A broad, elegant orange curve derived from the energy of a wagging tail | Adds direction, warmth, and a sense of forward movement | Page transitions, hero underlines, carousel motion, social frame edge, section dividers |
| **Soft Biomorphic Shapes** | Restrained rounded forms abstracted from ears, nose, and paw pads | Builds a recognisable background language without overusing the mascot | Background fields, pattern details, transition spaces, carousel backdrops |
| **Warm Porthole** | An off-white or charcoal circle that crops Oliver’s portrait | Creates consistency between full illustrations and compact placements | Portrait crops, author/quote cards, testimonials, profile imagery, social templates |

## Digital Rules

The system should lead with **one dominant device per composition**. A web hero may use the portrait crop and a single tail-swish; a social post may use the tag and a portrait crop. Avoid combining every device at once, because the mascot is already expressive and needs space to breathe.

| Context | Use | Keep clear | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Website hero** | Large Oliver crop at lower/right edge; left-side copy field; one orange swish to guide the eye to the CTA | At least 40% clear space for copy and conversion controls | Centre-aligning copy over Oliver, multiple badges, dense patterning |
| **Feature sections** | Small porthole portrait or tag marker; quiet grey forms in the background | Text hierarchy and button contrast | Repeating the full mascot in every section |
| **Social feed** | Top or upper-third copy field; playful portrait in lower half; one orange corner/tag accent | A consistent copy-safe zone across a series | Copy over the dog’s face, new unapproved colours, overly busy posts |
| **Stories/Reels covers** | Top and central blank fields; portrait anchored low; swish used as an edge frame | Safe zones for platform controls | Placing headlines at the extreme top/bottom, facial crops that are too tight |
| **Future merchandise** | Tag as embroidered patch, porthole portrait as badge, tail-swish as seam/sleeve detail | Flat, one- or two-colour reproduction | Using an intricate full illustration at very small sizes |

## Colour and Tone

Use the established palette with **orange as the action and energy colour**, not as a full-page default. Off-white should remain the primary breathing space; charcoal anchors the system; warm greys make the dog’s colours feel native to the environment.

| Role | Colour | Practical direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action / movement | `#E44D26` | Tag, ribbon, key marker, CTA accent |
| Anchor / contrast | `#333333` | Porthole fill, type, linework, dark controls |
| Mascot neutral | `#A6A19A` | Soft fields and character-adjacent details |
| Supporting neutral | `#817B75` | Secondary organic forms and tonal depth |
| Breathing space | `#F7F5F2` | Default canvas and text-safe areas |

## Recommended Next Step

Use the three starter assets as the first visual test across the homepage and a short social series. After the content purpose and core messaging are set, the next refinement should be a **small vector-ready asset library**: tag variations, approved tail-swish curves, porthole rules, and cropped Oliver expressions. That will make the system faster to deploy and suitable for merchandise production.

**Important:** These supplied images are creative visual directions and campaign-ready artwork. Before final production or trademark use, convert approved motifs into clean vector master files and confirm any legal clearance for the tag symbol.
